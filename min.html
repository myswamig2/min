01 · Purpose

Purify the Vessel. Govern the Power. Plant the Seed.

The Sacred Seed of Min is designed for consenting adult male initiates seeking to purify, restore, discipline and consecrate masculine generative power. Min (Mnw), associated in Khemet with fertility, harvest and the continuity of life, provides the sacred focal point.

Generative power extends beyond reproduction. Speech, teaching, service, productive labor, relationships, family and ancestral legacy all demand moral responsibility. The initiate places his intention beneath Ma’at — truth, balance, justice, reciprocity and right conduct.

I am not merely the possessor of the Sacred Seed. I am its guardian, cultivator and accountable steward.
02 · The documented teaching

Min in Ra Un Nefer Amen’s Metu Neter

Ra Un Nefer Amen expressly interprets Min in Metu Neter, Volume 1, in item 22 of his discussion of sacred images and symbols, on printed pages 408–409.

THE RAISED FLAIL

Discipline directed toward success

He associates the raised flail with the attainment of success, through discipline. The initiate cultivates steadiness, accountable conduct and purposeful effort.

THE PHALLIC EMBLEM

Vitality with moderation

He links Min’s fertility image with high libido, yet moderated sexual indulgence. The symbol is not a license for excess or exploitation.

The author's observation: Amen also places Min at the top of stairs receiving first-fruit offerings. Within his interpretation, disciplined strength and cultivated vitality culminate in a worthy harvest.[1]

Important distinction: Amen describes the symbolism of Min. The New Moon/Full Moon schedule, Sacred Seed stone, threefold water pouring, eleven ceremony steps and declarations below are contemporary SwamiG Institute ritual adaptations. They are not quotations from Amen or a claimed ancient Egyptian liturgy.

03 · Historical context

Min — Cultivation, Harvest and Continuity

Min was venerated particularly in Koptos (Gebtu) and Akhmim. His recognizable imagery includes high feathers, an upraised arm and flail, and a visible sign of fertility. Lettuce, cultivated crops and agricultural first fruits belong to the traditional context of his cult.

Amen-Min connects Min with the neter Amen in later Khemetic theology. The rite below responds to this symbolism through a modern ethic of self-governance, generative responsibility and care for what has been brought into being.

The ritual does not require nudity, sexual performance, sexual contact or the participation of another person. Its symbols, offerings, words and acts of cultivation carry the teaching.

Body · Symbol · Neter

I · THE BODY

The Living Vessel

The body embodies generative potential. Honor it through health, cleanliness, self-respect, respectful relationships and responsibility.

II · THE SYMBOL

The Sacred Min Vessel

A clean stone, carved emblem, ancestral cartouche or image of Min represents generative power held beneath a sacred intention.

III · THE NETER

Min (Mnw)

The neter of regeneration calls the initiate to create, nurture and safeguard what is worthy of enduring.

The Body carries potential. The Symbol preserves intention. The Neter reveals purpose.

06 · Sacred time

The Two Lunar Gates

Practice occurs at the New Moon and Full Moon. The preceding day, ritual day and following day form a three-day field of intentional speech, accountability, mindful work and reflection.

New Moon · Purify and Plant

Review unfinished obligations, release harmful patterns and choose a specific intention to cultivate.

Full Moon · Examine and Dedicate

Assess results, repair harms, give thanks for growth and recommit to sustaining what is worthy.

09 · Eleven-step ceremony

The Sacred Seed Ritual

1 Physical Cleansing

Bathe or shower using a gentle, body-safe cleanser. Wash the ritual stone separately with clean water and a soft cloth. Release shame, carelessness and disrespect for the body; renew the obligation to govern conduct.

My body is a vessel of life. My conduct determines how that life is honored.

2 White Cloth Preparation

Dress in clean white garments or wrap the body in a white cloth. Cover the stone with a second white cloth. Hold silence for several breaths and bring awareness to the heart.

3 Opening Invocation

Dua Min, ancient Neter of generation and renewal. Min of Koptos, Min of Akhmim, power of fertility, harvest and continuing life. May the vitality represented by your sacred image awaken within me as wisdom, discipline and responsible creativity. Amen, source of hidden possibility, guide my intention. Ma’at, bring my conduct into balance. Auset, teach me to nourish what is worthy of life. May I create without exploitation, protect without domination and lead without injustice. May the Sacred Seed become a blessing to the living, the ancestors and generations yet unborn.

4 Examination of Ma’at

At the New Moon, reflect on declarations 1–21 of the companion SGI Lingam ritual; at the Full Moon, reflect on declarations 22–42. Speak the seven Sacred Seed commitments:

  1. I will not confuse generative capacity with moral authority.
  2. I will not use sexuality, influence or spiritual office to exploit another.
  3. I will respect consent, dignity and personal autonomy.
  4. I will protect those affected by my actions.
  5. I will cultivate what I create rather than abandon it.
  6. I will seek accountability for harm rather than concealment.
  7. I will direct my strength toward life, wisdom and community benefit.

5 Light the Sacred Flame

Safely light the candle and contemplate potential, discipline and responsible creation.

Potential without direction is unfinished. Power without Ma’at is ungoverned. Creation without nurture is incomplete. May Min awaken my capacity to create what is worthy of continuation.

6 Threefold Consecration

Uncover the Sacred Seed stone. Position it over the bowl and pour clean water over it three times, allowing the water to collect in the bowl.

  1. Purification: I release deception, carelessness and misuse.
  2. Restoration: I restore disciplined creation and ancestral responsibility.
  3. Consecration: I dedicate my Sacred Seed to life, wisdom and righteous action.

7 Golden-Green Meditation

Breathe at a comfortable, natural rhythm. Envision golden light of awakened consciousness and green light of nourishment, vitality and compassionate responsibility becoming balanced within the body.

What I generate, I must consider. What I cultivate, I must nourish. What I bring into the world, I must answer for.

8 The Sacred Planting

Hold barley or wheat seeds in the palms and contemplate one worthy intention for the lunar cycle. Place the seeds in soil and water them modestly.

As this seed requires earth, water, warmth and care, so my intentions require discipline, labor, truth and patience. May I never mistake the planting of a seed for the completion of its purpose.

9 Offering to Min

Place lettuce before Min’s image, acknowledging his historical association with fertility and cultivated abundance.

Min, power of regeneration, receive this symbol of abundance. May the harvest of my life be measured not only by what I possess, but by what I cultivate for others. May my strength sustain life; may my knowledge produce wisdom; may my legacy extend beyond my lifetime.

10 Silence and Ancestral Reflection

Sit quietly. Consider the male ancestors whose experiences shaped your present. Recognize what deserves continuation and what must change. Legacy includes teaching, care, service and responsibility—not merely biological fatherhood.

I honor what my ancestors preserved. I transform what must not be repeated. I cultivate what future generations deserve to inherit.

11 Closing and Sealing

Dry the ritual stone and return it to the altar. Place the planting where it can be tended. Extinguish the flame safely.

By earth, I am grounded. By water, I am renewed. By fire, my intention is illuminated. By air, my intention is spoken. By Ma’at, my power is governed. By Min, may my creative potential be directed toward life. Dua Min. Dua Ma’at. Dua Amen. Ase.

This closing is original SGI ritual language, not a translation from an Egyptian temple text.

10 · Lunar declarations

Declarations for the Two Gates

New Moon · Prepare and Plant

I enter the darkness of the New Moon to examine the soil of my existence. I surrender destructive patterns and acknowledge neglected responsibilities. I purify my intentions before planting anew. May my Sacred Seed rest in disciplined potential until guided by wisdom. What must be released, I release. What must be repaired, I begin to repair. What is worthy of life, I prepare to cultivate.

Full Moon · Examine and Dedicate

I stand beneath the Full Moon in the light of accountability. I examine the consequences of what I have planted. Where my actions have produced good, I give thanks. Where my actions have caused harm, I accept the work of correction. I consecrate my generative power to truthful relationships, responsible leadership, productive labor and continuing life. May my harvest serve more than myself.
11 · Integration

Sacred Seed Journal

After the ceremony, record the date, lunar gate, intention, action and results.

  1. What have I released?
  2. What worthy intention have I planted?
  3. What practical action will nourish it?
  4. Where does my authority require accountability?
  5. How have I honored the ancestors?
  6. Who will benefit from my efforts?
  7. What has grown by the next lunar gate?

Commitment: Complete at least one observable act of care and responsibility before the next lunar ceremony.

13 · Boundaries

Consent, Privacy, Safety and Respect

Intended only for consenting adults who choose to participate. No sexual act, sexual contact, bodily fluids, sexual demonstration or physical contact with another person is required. Participation must not be coerced or treated as a condition of dignity or access to spiritual care.

Use candles and incense cautiously. Leave no flame unattended. Use clean, safe ritual materials. The ceremony is symbolic and is not medical treatment, diagnosis or a substitute for professional care.

Do not use initiatory status, sacred imagery or spiritual authority to pressure others. No one should be asked to surrender intimate information or perform for an audience.
